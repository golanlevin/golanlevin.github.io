## Biography

Golan Levin is an artist, researcher, educator, and instigator whose work explores computation as a cultural material. Through interactive systems, open-source tools, and media provocations, Levin applies creative twists to digital technologies that highlight our relationship with machines, expand the vocabulary of human action, and awaken participants to their own potential as creative actors.

Levin is [Professor of Computational Art in the School of Art](https://art.cmu.edu/people/golan-levin/) at Carnegie Mellon University, where he also holds affiliate appointments in the [School of Architecture](https://www.architecture.cmu.edu/profiles/golan-levin), [School of Design](https://www.design.cmu.edu/profiles/golan-levin), and [Computer Science Department](https://www.csd.cs.cmu.edu/people/faculty/golan-levin). His pedagogy is concerned with reclaiming computation as a medium of personal expression; he teaches “studio art courses in computer science” on themes like creative coding, generative AI, computational photography, machine perception, critical information visualization, experimental fabrication, and drawing with machines. Levin is co-author, with Tega Brain, of [*Code as Creative Medium*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262542043/code-as-creative-medium/) (MIT Press, 2021), a widely adopted guide to creative coding for software arts educators. He holds undergraduate and graduate degrees from the MIT Media Laboratory, where he studied in the Aesthetics and Computation Group.

Levin has spent more than 30 years as an artist working within high-technology research environments, including the MIT Media Laboratory, Interval Research Corporation, Ars Electronica Futurelab, and Eyebeam Center for Art and Technology. From 2009–2022 he served as Director of Carnegie Mellon's [Frank-Ratchye STUDIO for Creative Inquiry](https://studioforcreativeinquiry.org/), a laboratory dedicated to experimental research at the intersection of art, science, technology, and culture. During his thirteen-year tenure he expanded the STUDIO as a platform for interdisciplinary artistic research, overseeing grants supporting more than 500 projects, hosting over 70 artists-in-residence, organizing hundreds of public events, and raising more than \$2 million to support artist-led research initiatives. As Director, he established the STUDIO's [Frank-Ratchye Further Fund](https://studioforcreativeinquiry.org/frff-grants-microgrants), [Steiner Lecture Series](https://studioforcreativeinquiry.org/steiner-guests), and [Art && Code](https://index.artandcode.org/) conference series; renovated the STUDIO into a flexible interdisciplinary production space; and supported numerous arts investigations that received major international awards, premiered at leading museums and festivals, and helped shape emerging fields of interdisciplinary art.

Levin also develops open-source software, educational resources, and creative tools to make advanced computational techniques more accessible to artists, students, and researchers. In early 2010s, Levin participated as a member of the international [Free Art & Technology (F.A.T) Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Art_and_Technology_Lab) artist collective, through which he released projects like the [Free Universal Construction Kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Universal_Construction_Kit). More recent examples of Levin's open-source work include [PEmbroider](https://github.com/CreativeInquiry/PEmbroider) (a computational embroidery library for Processing) and [p5.plotSvg](https://github.com/golanlevin/p5.plotSvg/blob/main/README.md), a library for exporting plotter-friendly design files from p5.js. Currently, Levin is developing tools for machine perception, creative AI, experimental computer vision, and the recovery and documentation of obsolete creative technologies, treating reverse engineering as a mode of both creative practice and cultural stewardship.

Levin's artworks investigate the expressive possibilities of computation through interactive installations, generative systems, computational imaging, and participatory software. His work is included in the permanent collections of the [Museum of Modern Art](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/188560) (MoMA); the [Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum](https://collection.cooperhewitt.org/people/51679259/); and the [Zentrum für Kunst und Medientechnologie](https://zkm.de/en/artworks/messa-di-voce) (ZKM) in Karlsruhe, Germany, and has been exhibited internationally at venues including the Whitney Biennial, the New Museum of Contemporary Art, the Museum of Contemporary Art Taipei, Design Museum London, and the NTT InterCommunication Center (ICC) in Tokyo, among others. His work has been recognized with awards from Prix Ars Electronica, TR100, Webby Awards, *Core77* Design Awards, and *ID Magazine*'s Interactive Media Review, and has been supported by grants from the National Endowment for the Arts, the National Endowment for the Humanities, Creative Capital, the Rockefeller MAP Fund, and numerous public and private foundations. A two-time TED speaker, Levin was named one of *Fast Company*'s "50 Designers Shaping the Future."

## Academic Appointments & Employment

### Professor of Art, Carnegie Mellon University

* **Professor of Computational Art**, with Tenure. 9/2017–Present.  
* Associate Professor of Computational Art, with Tenure. 9/2010–8/2017.  
* Associate Professor of Computational Art. 9/2007–8/2010.
* Assistant Professor of Computational Art, 1/2004–8/2007.

I am a professor of computational and interactive new media arts in the School of Art at Carnegie Mellon University. Over the past two decades, much of my work at CMU has focused on building institutional capacity for artistic engagement with emerging technologies, and working to cultivate artists who are equally fluent in critical inquiry and technical practice. As an educator, my pedagogy is concerned with reclaiming computation as a medium of personal expression; I teach “studio art courses in computer science,” on themes like interactive art, generative AI, algorithmic form, critical information visualization, experimental fabrication, and audiovisual performance. My self-designed studio courses at Carnegie Mellon include: *Art and Generative AI*; *Creative Coding*; *Experimental Capture*; *Drawing with Machines*; *Interactive Art and Computational Design*; *Information Visualization as Art Practice*; *Audiovisual Systems and Machines*; and *Experimental Capture* (co-taught with Nica Ross). With Tega Brain, I am co-author of "Code as Creative Medium" (MIT Press, 2021), an educator's guide to creative coding.

My pedagogy is highly interdisciplinary. In addition to my home appointment [in the CMU School of Art](https://art.cmu.edu/people/golan-levin/), I also hold courtesy (affiliate) appointments in CMU's [School of Architecture](https://www.architecture.cmu.edu/profiles/golan-levin), [School of Design](https://design.cmu.edu/profiles/golan-levin), and [Computer Science Department](https://www.csd.cs.cmu.edu/people/faculty/golan-levin). Since 2008 I have served as the School of Art’s academic advisor for undergraduates pursuing two hybrid degree programs that I helped create: the Bachelor of Computer Science and Arts (BCSA) and the Bachelor of Engineering Studies and Arts (BESA). I also serve on the steering committee of, and teach courses in, CMU’s Integrative Design, Arts and Technology (IDeATe) Network, an interdepartmental consortium for undergraduate education in new media arts. In the service of these various appointments, I advise students from a wide range of programs, both formally and informally.

### **Director,** Frank-Ratchye STUDIO for Creative Inquiry

* **Director**, January 2009–September 2021
* **Co-Director**, September 2021–September 2022

From 2009-2022, I served as the Director of the [Frank-Ratchye STUDIO for Creative Inquiry](https://studioforcreativeinquiry.org/), the central research laboratory of CMU’s College of Fine Arts and a flexible site for atypical, anti-disciplinary, and inter-institutional research at the intersection of arts, science, technology and culture. My Directorship of the STUDIO was a 50% appointment, held alongside my professorship in the CMU School of Art. In the final year of my appointment, I co-directed the STUDIO with my successor.

Within the university, the STUDIO supports the arts-research of CMU’s faculty, staff, and students; organizes a wide range of public-facing activities, such as lectures, workshops, and conferences; publishes monographs, code and other open-source resources; and hosts artists-in-residence, with the objective of providing apprenticeship opportunities for students, and serving as a center of excellence in new forms of cultural production. The STUDIO’s emphasis on new media arts is built on nearly four decades of experience hosting interdisciplinary artists in an environment enriched by world-class science and engineering departments.

During my time as Director, I worked to expand the STUDIO as a platform for interdisciplinary arts research: overseeing grants supporting more than 500 projects, hosting over 70 artists-in-residence, organizing hundreds of public events, and raising more than \$2 million to support artist-led research initiatives. As Director, I established the STUDIO’s Frank-Ratchye Further Fund, Steiner Lecture Series, and Art && Code conference series; renovated the STUDIO into a flexible interdisciplinary production space; and supported numerous arts investigations that received major international awards, premiered at leading museums and festivals, and helped shape emerging fields of interdisciplinary art.
